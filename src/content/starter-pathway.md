## 4. Starter Pathway content

These are the canonical v1 lesson requirements. Copy may be polished for the GO voice, but the learning promise, mission, evidence, reflection, badge, skill, and XP must remain equivalent.

### World 1 — Notice

- **Learner promise:** Understand the repeating action inside a game.
- **Plain-language idea:** A game is a conversation between what the player does and what the rules do next.
- **Example to observe:** Pick any familiar game and notice one small loop: choose, act, receive a result, choose again.
- **Practical mission:** Draw or describe one game loop in three to five steps.
- **Evidence:** A loop sketch, short written sequence, voice note, or photo of a paper diagram.
- **Reflection:** What is the smallest action that makes this loop begin?
- **Achievement:** `signal-seeker` — Signal Seeker.
- **Skill:** `observe-game-systems` — Observe game systems.
- **Mission XP:** 50.
- **Next route:** Foundations / Imagine.
- **Modes:** drawing, paper-prototype, no-code, level-editor, game-engine, programming.

### World 2 — Imagine

- **Learner promise:** Turn a large idea into a clear player experience.
- **Plain-language idea:** A strong game idea can be stated as what the player gets to do and feel.
- **Example to observe:** “The player gets to sneak a tiny robot past sleeping machines.”
- **Practical mission:** Complete “The player gets to ___” and name one action the player takes.
- **Evidence:** The one-sentence player promise plus the chosen action.
- **Reflection:** If only one thing remains playable, what should it be?
- **Achievement:** `idea-orbit` — Idea Orbit.
- **Skill:** `define-player-experience` — Define player experience.
- **Mission XP:** 50.
- **Next route:** Shape / Rules.
- **Modes:** drawing, paper-prototype, no-code, level-editor, game-engine, programming.

### World 3 — Shape

- **Learner promise:** Shape a small, playable ruleset.
- **Plain-language idea:** Rules are promises about what can happen, what matters, and how the player learns the result.
- **Example to observe:** In a tiny challenge, identify its goal, constraints, choice, feedback, and change.
- **Practical mission:** Write one goal, three rules, one meaningful choice, one feedback signal, and one thing that changes.
- **Evidence:** A rules card, diagram, paper prototype, or short design brief.
- **Reflection:** Which rule creates a useful decision, and which part is only decoration?
- **Achievement:** `scope-scout` — Scope Scout.
- **Skill:** `design-rules-and-scope` — Design rules and scope.
- **Mission XP:** 75.
- **Next route:** Build / Prototype.
- **Modes:** drawing, paper-prototype, no-code, level-editor, game-engine, programming.

### World 4 — Build

- **Learner promise:** Build a tiny playable version.
- **Plain-language idea:** A prototype is an experiment that lets someone experience the important part.
- **Example to observe:** Compare a one-minute paper game or gray-box level with its larger imagined version. Notice what was removed.
- **Practical mission:** Make a loop that can be played in under five minutes. Remove anything that does not help the loop.
- **Evidence:** A playable prototype, paper game, screen recording, project file, or sequence of screenshots.
- **Reflection:** What did you remove so the important action could be felt sooner?
- **Achievement:** `builder-spark` — Builder Spark.
- **Skill:** `prototype-playable-systems` — Prototype playable systems.
- **Mission XP:** 100.
- **Next route:** Playtest Lab / Test.
- **Modes:** drawing, paper-prototype, no-code, level-editor, game-engine, programming.

### World 5 — Test

- **Learner promise:** Learn what the game communicates without explaining it for the player.
- **Plain-language idea:** Playtesting turns another person’s actions into design information.
- **Example to observe:** Watch a player pause, repeat an action, miss a signal, smile, ask a question, or find a surprising solution.
- **Practical mission:** Ask one person to try the prototype. Stay quiet for the first round. Record three observations and make one change.
- **Evidence:** Three observations plus the revision made in response.
- **Reflection:** What did the player teach you that you could not have learned by staring at the prototype alone?
- **Achievement:** `playtest-oracle` — Playtest Oracle.
- **Skill:** `observe-players-and-improve` — Observe players and improve.
- **Mission XP:** 100.
- **Next route:** Share / Release Lab.
- **Modes:** paper-prototype, no-code, level-editor, game-engine, programming.

### World 6 — Share

- **Learner promise:** Explain what you made, what you learned, and what happens next.
- **Plain-language idea:** Sharing is part of making: it helps another person understand the experiment and gives the work a next life.
- **Example to observe:** A clear project note answers what it is, how to try it, what changed, and what the creator would do next.
- **Practical mission:** Write a creator note with **Name / Play / Learn / Next**, then attach a link, file, photo, or video if available.
- **Evidence:** A project page, presentation, creator note, or shareable prototype record.
- **Reflection:** What is the smallest next step that would make this project more interesting?
- **Achievement:** `signal-sender` — Signal Sender.
- **Skill:** `communicate-and-present` — Communicate and present.
- **Mission XP:** 125.
- **Next route:** GO Projects / Release Lab / Foundations.
- **Modes:** drawing, paper-prototype, no-code, level-editor, game-engine, programming.

### Pathway completion

When all six stable lesson IDs are completed:

- award `starter-game-maker` — Starter Game Maker;
- create or update the six Passport skills;
- create one public completion record if the learner's existing profile visibility rules permit it;
- store the project evidence link or summary only when the learner explicitly chooses to share it;
- award a one-time pathway completion bonus of 100 XP.

The six mission values sum to **500 mission XP**. With the pathway bonus, the lifetime total from this pathway is **600 XP**. Product copy should say “500 mission XP + 100 completion bonus” to avoid the common ambiguity between “500 total” and the bonus.
